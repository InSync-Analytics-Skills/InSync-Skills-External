# Writing `narrative.json`

Read this file once per run, after `contract.json` exists and before the build. It is the only
part of the workbook you write; every figure in it must come from `contract.json`, and the builder
checks that.

## The panel you are writing for

   - **Right of the table, the buy-side panel: FIVE sections, curated conclusions only.** A phase status banner, then: **1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY** — the `Result:` line, then AT MOST FOUR bullets that read as ONE CONNECTED INVESTMENT STORY, in this order: what changed → what financially drove it → revision quality / what confirms or contradicts it → the `Key Debate.` line. Never four independent statistics: each bullet must follow from the one before it, and an observation that does not materially change the interpretation is cut, however interesting. **ONE analytical conclusion per bullet** — never combine unrelated analytical dimensions (revision quality + operating evidence + share performance) into one sentence merely because they fit the character limit; a bullet doing three jobs is three observations wearing one sentence. The slot ranking is fixed: Revision Quality > Contradiction > Market Reaction, and Market Reaction takes a slot only when the price move itself changes the thesis (estimates +5% with the stock −12% qualifies; +2% against the index +1% never does) → **2. ESTIMATE REVISIONS** — at most 3 upgrades and 2 downgrades (maximums, not quotas; honest empty state on either side: `No material upgrades identified (2Q27).` / `No material downgrades identified (2Q27).`), then three label+data blocks: *What drove the revision* (Financial drivers — the earnings bridge — then one `Forward evidence (2Q27):` line of operating KPIs, then `Cash flow (2Q27).`), *Revision path (2Q27)*, *What consensus embeds*. ⛔ **Every quarter-only block names its forecast period in its label** (`Upgrades (2Q27)`, `Downgrades (2Q27)`, `Financial drivers: contribution to the 2Q27 PBT revision`, `Revenue mix (2Q27)`) — the reader must never infer which period a figure belongs to; the horizon ladder, the divergence line and every narrative sentence carry their own period labels. The house period form is `2Q27` / `FY2027`, no `E` suffix (2–4 assumptions that define the bar; cycle rank only when extreme) → **3. KEY DEBATE: VARIANT VIEW** — the house FOUR pairs, in order: `Street View.` (what the evidence supports), `What Changed.` (the bound contradiction when one binds, else the headline move), `Potential Variant.` (the specific assumption the market may misunderstand), `Variant Assessment.` (the judgment, opening with the fixed verdict vocabulary: `Supported.` / `Partially Supported.` / `Not Supported.` / `Insufficient Evidence.` — the last is a legitimate first-class outcome, never auto-softened to a middle verdict, and it is the honest fallback when no assessment is written) → **4. EARNINGS EXPECTATIONS** (heading FIXED across every cycle phase — the phase changes the analytical job, never the section's name) — three OUTCOME blocks `Base` / `Extend` / `Challenge`, each a measurable threshold line plus a one-sentence implication (Base validates the current consensus, Extend would extend the revision, Challenge would put it at risk) → **5. KEY MONITORING VARIABLES** (max 3, each `Fact:` / `Monitor:` — see the KM metric-integrity rule).

   - ⛔ **Do not add a sixth section.** Every earlier draft grew by bolting on a block; the panel was cut from nine sections to five on purpose. A new finding belongs *inside* one of the five, or nowhere.

   - ⛔ **TWO-TIER HOME RULE for figures.** The Executive Summary is the home of the HEADLINE figures — a summary repeats by design, so it may carry the small set (≈5–6) needed to tell the whole story. Below it, every figure has ONE home in the detail tier, and a detail section never restates an exec-summary figure — it DECOMPOSES it (exec: "Segment A 80% of the revision"; drivers: "Unit X 45% | Unit Y 35%" without re-quoting the 80%). A repeat is permitted only when it serves a different analytical purpose or a section would be unintelligible without it — the guard is against unnecessary/verbatim repetition, not repetition itself. The builder prints a `⚠ detail-tier figures repeated 3+ times` warning (exec rows exempt); treat it as a defect unless each repeat demonstrably earns its place.

   - **Length is capped and enforced at render.** Thesis ≤230 chars, every bullet ≤122 (one panel row), one idea per bullet. The renderer cuts a labelled line or the thesis at 230 characters and a plain bullet at 122, and the build log names every cut and the text it lost (`⚠ WARNING: text cut at the length cap`) plus every narrative line that wraps past one panel row, measured as label + text (`⚠ … narrative line(s) wrap past one panel row`): treat both as rewrite instructions. The builder's own computed lines (Cycle rank, Read-through) are never reported. **Note the real lever: most apparent "length" is WRAPPING, not verbosity.** The panel's line budget is 136 characters (`PW`) against a 122-char one-idea bullet cap, so a 150-char bullet silently becomes two rows. Fix length by tightening the sentence to one row, not by deleting the fact.

   - ⛔ **Coverage/dispersion is machinery — NEVER a visible section.** `intel.disagreement` (broker counts, ranges, quarterly-vs-annual coverage via `annCnt`) is still computed in full, but it surfaces ONLY when the coverage gap materially changes CONFIDENCE in the interpretation — the bar is a genuine asymmetry (the contradiction side priced by less than about a third of headline coverage, or a single-digit count against a deep book), stated inside the `Potential Variant.` argument. A routine depth difference (12 vs 35) is internal evidence and stays in the contract; coverage counts are never default narrative content. A standalone depth/coverage block reads as the report explaining its dataset's limitations instead of drawing a conclusion. The quarterly-vs-annual asymmetry remains the prize finding: 3 quarterly vs 9 annual estimates means the Street prices the year more confidently than the ramp within it — a timing-precision statement, never "only 3 brokers, therefore weak".

   - **The machinery is computed but NOT displayed.** Breadth, velocity, persistence, reversal counts, scores and dispersion percentiles live in the contract and select what appears; they never appear as visible output. A machinery number may surface only as *evidence inside an argument*, never as a standalone statistic, and a broker or analyst COUNT only inside the Variant when the coverage gap IS the argument (see the coverage/dispersion rule above); anywhere else the build lint flags it.

   - **The framing is set by the computed earnings-cycle PHASE, not by the writer.** `intel.phase` is derived from days-since-print and days-to-next-print and mandates the central question, the framing and the analytical job of the outcome section (`phase.job`); the section titles stay the same in every phase: `pre` (next print ≤21 days — the BAR) → `fresh` (≤14 days since print — the RESET) → `digest` (≤45 days — DURABILITY) → `late` (CARRY-FORWARD). An imminent print outranks every other phase. Follow `phase.framing`; do not re-frame by feel.

**Scannable, not paragraph-heavy.** The detail sections favour `Label: number, conclusion` lines over prose — a reader should scan Financial Drivers, Revision Path, Consensus Embeds, the Variant, Expectations and KM in seconds and hold the whole argument. Prose belongs in the Executive Summary and the Variant argument, nowhere else.

**Curate, don't enumerate.** The panel's job is "the few things that matter from the analysis", never "all the analysis we performed". Every visible element must answer one of: what changed / why / how significant / how far it extends / what consensus now expects / where the debate is / what outcome validates, extends or challenges it / what to watch next. If a computed result does not materially help answer one of these, it stays in `contract.json`. Do NOT optimise for a line count: the target is maximum signal per line — a line earns its place by adding distinct investment value, and content is never cut merely to hit a number.

## House voice — this is an InSync research product

The Revision workbook sits beside Earnings Preview, Flash, Wrap and Review. It must read as the same product line: concise, factual, evidence-led, institutional. **Let the data make the point.** The reader is a buy-side analyst who will draw their own conclusion if you put the number in front of them; your job is to put the right number there, not to tell them what to feel about it.

**The discipline: Fact → Change → Evidence → Why It Matters**, and it belongs INSIDE the sentence. State the metric and its level first; the inference is a short trailing clause introduced by *so*, *against*, or *while* — never a standalone sentence of opinion.

> ✅ `Revenue of $10.42bn came in $0.15bn above consensus and declined 1.4% Y/Y, so the earnings beat did not come from volume.`
> ✅ `COGS (Ex-1x) rose 5.0% against revenue up 5.0% and Opex 2.9%, so gross margin held.`
> ❌ `The Street raised a chip designer hard into this print.`  ❌ `That is the whole bear case.`  ❌ `The risk is the bar, not the fundamentals.`

⛔ **Match the verb to the strength of the evidence.** Use `shows`, `rose`, `fell`, `widened`, `narrowed` for what the data states directly. Use `suggests`, `points to`, `implies`, `raises the possibility` for what the analysis reads into it. An unhedged mechanism ("driven by", "due to", "reflects", "a timing shift") is flagged at build time unless the sentence hedges. Sounding analytical beats sounding certain.

⛔ **State what the data shows, not why it happened.** A cross-horizon divergence is the clearest trap: the numbers prove that FCF is up for the quarter and down for the year, and prove nothing about whether that is timing or deterioration. Write `The improvement does not carry through the full year`, never `a timing shift rather than a change in level`. The same test applies to every computed finding — if the sentence contains a mechanism, ask which figure established it.

⛔ **Never assert causality the data does not establish, and never infer valuation.** Two failure modes seen in review: (a) writing that an outer-year revision is *driven by* a thinly-covered KPI — the coverage and the revision are both facts, the causal link between them is not; (b) explaining a price/revision gap as "multiple compression" — this skill holds no valuation data, so describe the divergence and stop. Say what moved and what is thinly covered; let the reader connect them.

⛔ **Every rule in this skill is a GENERAL rule.** When a company exposes a weakness, the fix is a selection, ownership or narrative rule that applies to every ticker, sector and cycle phase. Never a ticker-specific exception. The test before adding anything: *can this be written as a rule that improves the output for any company?* If not, it does not belong in the skill. Two consequences worth knowing in advance: a general rule will sometimes overturn a decision that looked right on one name, and the same rule set must be able to produce two completely different reports without either looking like a template.

⛔ **A metric earns its place or it is not shown.** A line changing is not a reason to display it. It appears only if it does at least one of: materially explains the revision, changes how the revision reads, creates a contradiction, defines an expectation consensus now carries, or can validate or invalidate the thesis. Everything else stays in the contract for audit.

⛔ **Show the conclusion, not the machinery.** The engine is allowed to be complex; the panel is not allowed to show its workings. Broker counts, agreement counts ("5 of 5 core metrics"), breadth counts ("25 of 30 tracked lines"), contribution shares over 100% ("139%, offset elsewhere"), coverage statistics, dispersion labels and small-sample cycle ranks all SELECT evidence, rank contradictions, set confidence and decide wording — none of them is a finding. Print what the analysis concluded, not how it concluded it. The single exception: when a coverage or dispersion gap IS the investment insight, it belongs in the Variant as part of the argument (a next-platform ramp priced by 9 annual brokers against 3 on quarterly timing is the argument, so it is stated; a "0.6% broker range" behind a software step is machinery, so it is not). A build-time lint flags the patterns above outside that exception.

**Every sentence carries a number.** A sentence with no figure in it is an opinion and does not belong on the panel. If a claim cannot be expressed with a computed figure, it is a watch item, not a finding.

**Banned register:** dramatic or conversational phrasing ("raised hard", "the whole bear case", "essentially all the work", "nobody covers", "close to meaningless", "the market was right first"), rhetorical questions in body text, second-person address, and any verdict the numbers do not force. Superlatives are permitted only when computed and stated as a rank ("ranks 2 of the last 5 cycles"), never as colour.

**House conventions to match (shared with Wrap, Preview Portal and Tear Sheet, and machine-checked in all three):**

- ⛔ **No em dash in prose.** `eval_tearsheet.py` REJECTS a payload containing one; the Preview Portal locks the same rule. Use a comma, a colon or a period. En-dash ranges (`$194–199B`, `50k–1,857k`) are fine, and the table missing-data glyph is unaffected. **No semicolons** — split the sentence.
- **Bullets are `**Label.** clause`** — bolded noun label, a period, then the clause; bold on the label only. The renderer emits this as rich-text runs (`pLbl`), so never hand-build a label with a dash separator.
- **Sentences ≤30 words**, one observation each, fact first and interpretation second. **One analytical idea per sentence:** more than three figures in a prose sentence is a data dump and is flagged. Data rows (`Label:` with values, or a `|`-separated series) are exempt — they are not sentences. (The Wrap's stricter one-comma rule is deliberately NOT adopted here: a Fact → Change → Evidence line is one sentence by design and the density is the product.)
- **Watch items use the `Fact:` / `Monitor:` pair** (Wrap §9 Key Monitoring Variables). `Fact` establishes the current bar or the unresolved evidence; `Monitor` states the OBSERVATION that would resolve the debate the Variant opened — the chain is Variant → Expectations → Monitoring, never three independently interesting KPIs, and never a KPI merely because it moved. Expectations test the print; Monitors watch beyond it — the same variable may appear in both only when the monitor extends the test forward (next-quarter pace, FY carry, the guide). **KM METRIC INTEGRITY:** the item's title, Fact and Monitor must refer to the SAME metric or explicitly defined family — never a "Quarter Bookings" title over an RPO-additions monitor (build-time lint checks the title's words appear in the Fact/Monitor text). Legacy `{watch, why}` still renders — `why` maps to `Fact`, `watch` to `Monitor`, never the reverse.
- **Verdict vocabulary is fixed:** `Supported` / `Partially Supported` / `Not Supported` / `Insufficient Evidence`. Not "Partly supported". `Insufficient Evidence` is a legitimate outcome — never force a judgment the evidence cannot carry.
- **The Key Debate is phrased `Whether …`**, never `Does …?`.
- **Banned lexicon** (ported from `validate_wrap.py`): attractive, compelling, undervalued, overvalued, investors should, overweight, underweight, price target, upside potential, buy, sell, plus the opinion phrases (future optionality, investors believe, the market thinks, premium/cheap/rich valuation, priced for perfection, premium/discount to peers).
- **The price move has ONE home.** Market Reaction when the Executive Summary renders it, otherwise inside the Variant argument it corroborates; What Consensus Embeds carries no price line (Wrap content rule 4: state the reaction exactly once).
- Units follow the family: `$88.4bn`, `+860bp`, `+8.4pp`, `91.7% Y/Y`, `11.8% sequential`. A bare figure keeps its sign (`Services −$940m`); after a verb that already carries the direction the amount is unsigned (`was cut $513m`, `took $222m from PBT`, never `cut −$513m` or `added +$1.2bn`) — `references/sanity_check.py` fails a panel that doubles the sign. Consensus is "InSync consensus" or "consensus", never "analysts think". The sheet prints no source line (the no-footer rule in `references/script-rules.md`); when the run summary in chat quotes figures, it closes with `Source: InSync MCP.`

## The keys

Write `narrative.json` after reading `contract.json`, before the build. Read `intel.phase.framing`, `intel.dataState` and `intel.thesisSpine` FIRST and write every section to that frame. When `intel.dataState.key` is not `ok` (no post-print update, or no pre-print anchor) the `Result:` line states that fact and nothing on the panel may describe a 0.0% move as a revision: the band note and the muted `–` cells own it, and the computed fallbacks already stand down. If `intel.contradiction.binding` is true, the `variant` must argue that contradiction.

- `thesis` — the `Result:` line: what consensus did, with the figures.
- `summary` — the Executive Summary's labelled bullets. **THE ONE AUTHORITATIVE BULLET RULE: the Executive Summary renders the `Result:` line, then AT MOST FOUR bullets total — a ceiling, never a quota — of which the `Key Debate.` line is always the FINAL bullet, so at most three labelled content bullets render, and they must chain into one argument** (what changed → what drove it → how good it is), never independent statistics.
  - `whatChanged`: the conclusion about the estimate picture, with any interpretation attached to its evidence.
  - `whatDroveIt`: the FINANCIAL contributors behind it (the engine's levers), never an operating KPI dressed as an earnings contributor.
  - `quality`: the revision-quality read from `intel.thesisSpine.qualityFlags` and `intel.evidence`: is the driver durable, does the operating evidence confirm or contradict it.
  - `marketReaction` (conditional) COMPETES with `quality` for the third slot. When a contradiction binds it yields: the price gap belongs inside the Variant Assessment. It earns a slot only when nothing binds and the gap is real (the Market Reaction rule below), or you supply it deliberately; when both matter, fold the price into `quality`. Supply ONE string; the `{positive, negative, net}` triplet still renders, but only when the three lines say three different things.
  - There is NO standalone "What It Means" bullet: an evidence-free line of interpretation ("consensus is paying up for momentum") is the register this product bans. Never pad to the maximum bullet count.
  - **The evidence budget is 4–6 figures across the whole summary** (linted above 8): the smallest set of numbers that tells the entire story, chosen for analytical importance, never availability.
- `street` / `variantChange` / `variant` / `assessment` plus `verdict` — the Variant View's FOUR house pairs in order (Street View, What Changed, Potential Variant, Variant Assessment). `verdict` is one of `Supported` / `Partially Supported` / `Not Supported` / `Insufficient Evidence` (never force a conclusion the evidence cannot carry); `assessment` is its one evidence line. Potential Variant frames what the market may MISUNDERSTAND, never a restatement of the results.
- `keyQuestion` — phrased `Whether …`; it renders as the Executive Summary's `Key Debate.` pair.
- `base` / `extend` / `challenge` — each `{threshold, implication}`: the threshold line carries the measurable numbers (separated by `|` when there are two), the implication says in one short sentence what that outcome means for the revision. A plain string still renders as one line for older narratives. Outcomes are tied to this company's actual revisions, never a generic bull/base/bear.
  - **Each must name a measurable consensus threshold** — the level, percentage or spread the outcome turns on. "Results in line with revised expectations" is not an outcome when the consensus number is sitting in the contract; a date is not a threshold.
  - **State thresholds as bare levels** ("SSS (Ex-FX) for 2Q26 below −5.0%", "Revenue above $2,000m"), never as "the consensus low/high" or "the broker range": that trips the coverage-statistic lint, and range provenance is machinery.
  - **ONE primary test per outcome**: a second threshold may join only when it tests the SAME hypothesis (revenue above the high plus the bookings level that confirms it is one test of demand); two unrelated hypotheses are two outcomes, so keep the one with the highest Variant sensitivity.
- `agenda` — max 3 × `{title, fact, monitor}`. A watch item is **a variable and a test, not a topic**: not "new-product ramp" but "Segment B revenue against the $10bn now in consensus". `fact` carries the computed number (the current bar or the unresolved evidence); `monitor` is the observation that would RESOLVE the Variant's debate and may be qualitative in form (an imperative is not a claim). The chain is Variant → Expectations → Monitoring, so at least one item watches the Variant's own resolution; never pick a KPI merely because it moved.
  - **Expectations and Monitoring are different tenses**: an Expectation is what the PRINT must deliver; a Monitor is what to watch AFTER and beyond it (the next-quarter pace, the FY carry, the guide). A monitor may track an outcome's variable only by extending it forward, never by repeating the at-the-print test.
  - **No broker counts, ranges or coverage statistics in a watch item**, unless the coverage gap is the bound Variant argument, in which case the Variant states it and the watch item does not repeat it.
- Legacy keys (`takeaway`, `bull`/`bear`/`variantAssessment`/`bullNeeds`/`bearIf`, agenda `{watch, why}` or `{fact, question}`) still render via fallbacks; new runs write the current schema.

Do NOT write a "where consensus breaks" section: `intel.disagreement` (broker coverage vs range width) is machinery that selects evidence and is never rendered as a section (see the coverage/dispersion rule).

**Fencing rules.** Cite ONLY figures present in `contract.json` (intel block included). Ground every "why" in guidance/transcript facts you actually fetched; when you have not fetched them, frame the driver as a question for the agenda, never as an assertion. No generic filler ("macro risks", "competition"): every sentence names a specific computed change. Company-specific vocabulary comes from the segment labels in the contract, not from your general knowledge of the company.

## Information architecture — what each section is FOR

Analysis alone produces "here are ten interesting things, spread across eight blocks", and that is what makes a report feel repetitive even when every line is true. Between the analysis and the render sits one more layer: **every section has a job, and a conclusion may only reappear if the next section does a different job with it.**

**The pipeline:** `raw data → findings → revision thesis → roles → conditional routing → render`.

**THE ANALYTICAL SPINE.** Before any section is written, the report internally constructs one chain for every company, whatever the industry: **REVISION** (what changed) → **FINANCIAL DRIVER** (what mathematically moved earnings — the engine) → **QUALITY** (is the driver durable — margin direction, concentration, cash leg, operating evidence) → **CONTRADICTION** (what evidence disagrees — `intel.contradictions`, led by financial-revision-vs-operating-evidence) → **VARIANT** (where consensus could be wrong) → **EXPECTATION** (the measurable number that resolves it) → **MONITOR** (what to watch next). Every right-side section is an expression of this spine; only the economic drivers change by sub-industry. The visible output carries the conclusions, never the machinery that reached them.

**Section jobs** (the renderer tags each block with its role; the audit measures against these):

| Section | Role | Job |
|---|---|---|
| Executive Summary | `summary` | Tell the complete revision story: `Result:`, then **at most FOUR bullets total (ceiling, not quota; the one authoritative bullet rule)** — up to three labelled bullets drawn from **What Changed.** / **What Drove It.** / **Revision Quality.** / **Market Reaction.**, with **Key Debate.** always the final bullet. Not every label appears. Exempt from the repetition rules by design. |
| Estimate Revisions | `finding` | The most economically important changes. Curated (≤3 up, ≤2 down), not enumerated. |
| What Drove the Revision | `decomposition` | TWO layers, never mixed: **Financial drivers** — the earnings-bridge contributors and offsets, largest first — then one **Forward evidence** line of operating KPIs (confirming and contradicting) that test the revision's durability. No shares, no debate, no outlook, no price. |
| Revision Path | `behaviour` | WHEN the move landed and whether it held. |
| What Consensus Embeds | `threshold` | ONE job: the 2–4 assumptions that DEFINE the current bar — never a data inventory. Growth and level, the horizon ladder, a divergence that qualifies them. Key levers, cycle rank, the `Read-through:` line and the horizon ladder are computed and worded by the builder (`references/script-rules.md`). No price, no revision-path mechanics. |
| Key Debate: Variant View | `insight` | The house FOUR pairs: **Street View.** / **What Changed.** / **Potential Variant.** / **Variant Assessment.** Where the headline reading is incomplete, vulnerable or contradicted. |
| Earnings Expectations | `outcome` | Base / Extend / Challenge as **outcome → threshold → implication**, on separate lines. The strongest section on the sheet: an analyst should finish it knowing the exact number that decides the next cycle. |
| Key Monitoring Variables | `action` | Specific unresolved variables as the house **Fact: / Monitor:** pair — the computed anchor, then the forward test. |

**Market Reaction is conditional, and size alone is not the condition.** It appears only when the price move changes how the revision reads. Two qualifying cases, both computed: the move CONTRADICTS the revision (opposite sign, at least 3pp against the index), or it REPRICES on a scale that is itself the story (15pp or more in the same direction). "Estimates rose and the shares rose" is not a finding on any ticker. A writer-supplied line still renders, so judgment can override the test.

**Base, Extend and Challenge RESOLVE the Variant.** Identify the central tension first, then write the three outcomes so each answers "if this happens, what does it tell us about the Variant?": **Base** = the evidence validates the Street view; **Extend** = the evidence proves the Street is still underestimating the driver; **Challenge** = the evidence invalidates or materially weakens the Street's core assumption. At least one threshold must directly test the contradiction the Variant argues (build-time lint). A generic beat/miss template ("beats revenue, raises guidance") is not an outcome — it would read identically for every company in the index.

**Monitoring variables must be UNRESOLVED.** They are not a place to restate a conclusion already established elsewhere. Select the three variables with the highest ability to validate, extend, challenge or break the thesis, and let the data choose them: pricing and inventory for one company, ramp timing and working capital for another. The framework is fixed, the variables are not.

**Section identity is fixed; the analytical job adapts to the cycle phase.** A reader moving between a pre-print and a post-print report must recognise the same product, so `EARNINGS EXPECTATIONS` keeps its heading whether the phase is pre, fresh, digest or late. What changes is `intel.phase.job`: the same three outcomes define what the upcoming print must deliver (pre), whether the initial reset holds (fresh), what must happen before the next catalyst (digest), or what consensus still needs to see (late).

**Ownership is conditional, not fixed.** A fact belongs wherever it is currently load-bearing. The price/revision gap sits in `threshold` when the market is confirming the revision and moves to `insight` when it contradicts it. Do not hard-ban a fact from a section; ask what job it is doing in this particular report.

**The transform rule (this replaces "one number, one home").** A *claim* is one subject carrying one figure. The same **subject** should recur — that is how a report builds an argument. The same **claim** may not be restated role after role; it must be transformed:

> `finding` Segment B −3.5% → `decomposition` offset −$600m → `insight` the upgrade is concentrated outside Segment B → `outcome` Segment B above/below $10bn decides whether the revision broadens → `action` watch Segment B revenue vs $10bn.

Each use adds something the previous one did not. The build audit flags any claim appearing in three or more roles.

⛔ **FINANCIAL DRIVERS vs OPERATING EVIDENCE — never mix the two.** An operating KPI — RPO, billings, bookings, backlog, deferred revenue, deliveries, units, traffic, customers, NRR — is NEVER presented as a PBT/EPS contributor unless a defensible quantitative earnings bridge exists (an underwriting ratio on its premium base is such a bridge; an RPO dollar move is not). Operating KPIs enter the drivers block only through the single `Forward evidence:` line, where `intel.evidence` sorts each material KPI move into CONFIRMING or CONTRADICTING the revision; one highly relevant contradicting indicator outweighs several low-relevance confirming ones.

⛔ **RESIDUALS ARE MACHINERY, never an economic driver.** The consensus-mix residual reconciles the bridge internally and stays in `contract.json`; it never prints ("Other / mix +$668m" reads as a business driver the analyst cannot act on). A residual may surface only if it has a defined business interpretation.

**Revision quality is the second question every report answers.** Not just "up or down?" but "how good?" — broad improvement in earnings power versus mix, timing, assumptions, tax or capital allocation. `intel.thesisSpine.qualityFlags` computes the ingredients (driver concentration, margin direction, annual cash leg, whether the dominant lever is the top line); the writer carries them through the Executive Summary, Variant View and Assessment — never as a new section.

**Horizon consistency.** A quarterly and an annual figure never share a sentence unless each carries its own horizon label ("3.1% for 3Q26 and 4.9% for FY2026") or the cross-horizon comparison is explicitly the finding. Linted at build time.

The **Key Debate poses two interpretations** ("Whether X, or Y" — linted): not "will they beat?" but "is the revision evidence of stronger earnings power, or of a less durable driver?".

**Never force the slots.** Three upgrades and two downgrades are MAXIMUMS: an auto insurer's single material upgrade (`Loss Ratio −90bp`) stands alone, and "No material downgrades identified" beats a manufactured third item. The same applies to monitors: up to three, chosen by thesis sensitivity (materiality × uncertainty × ability to change the conclusion × observability at the next catalyst) — two strong items beat two strong items plus filler.

**Street View states what consensus BELIEVES, never the headline again.** The Executive Summary owns the headline numbers; a Street View repeating two or more of its figures is linted. Write the belief ("consensus is underwriting continued loss-ratio improvement rather than premium growth"), then let What Changed and the Variant argue with it. **Key levers in Consensus Embeds** print the LEVELS of the top engine levers (ratio levers as the ratio, cost levers as the derived margin), skipping any figure that already anchors a Base/Extend/Challenge threshold.

**What Changed is the conclusion; What Drove It is the evidence.** These two labels are the easiest place in the report to say the same thing twice. *What Changed* describes the change in the estimate picture ("the upgrade shifted toward Segment A while Segment B, margin and annual FCF moved the other way"). *What Drove It* shows the contributors and offsets that produce it ("Segment A added $1.3bn while Segment B took out $600m"). If either could be swapped for the other, one of them is wrong.

**The revision thesis.** `intel.thesisSpine` computes the ingredients an honest thesis cannot omit — the headline move, the dominant driver, and the dominant contradiction when one binds. The driver FOLLOWS THE DIRECTION of the segment revision (`intel.attribution.driver`; `offset` is the largest move against it): the driver of a revenue cut is the segment cut most, never the one segment that rose (Segment B −$900m, not Segment A +$200m). The same rule words the mix contradiction (`The cut is carried by …`) and the offset sentence under Revenue mix, which says `more than offset` only when the gain is the larger side and `The reduction in X outweighed the gain in Y` otherwise; a mix line whose % rounds to zero is not printed. The *sentence* is written, never generated: a computed thesis reads like a template, and this is the one line on the sheet that must not. The audit checks the executive summary references every spine ingredient and warns on omission.

**Build-time audit.** `build_revision_xlsx.py` prints the checks below and never edits output. The last group is ported straight from the sibling products, so the Revision panel is held to the same standard `validate_wrap.py` and `eval_tearsheet.py` enforce rather than to a private house style: em dash and semicolon in prose, banned lexicon, unquantified movement claims, sentences over 30 words, and repeated bullet openings (data rows such as the quarter and FY mix lines repeat their label by design and are exempt). Wrapped visual lines are reassembled into logical bullets first, so a number that landed on the previous line still counts. The five architecture checks are: claim repetition across roles, thesis-spine coverage, variant subject vs the bound contradiction, Base/Extend/Challenge naming a measurable threshold, and watch items naming a test rather than a topic. Before all of these, the **narrative figure check** compares every amount, %, bp/pp move, ratio and count in `narrative.json` with the figures in `contract.json`, at the precision written (row labels, dates, years and period labels are skipped); a figure it cannot find is a fabricated or mistyped number. A clean build prints only the line count. **Treat any ⚠ as a rewrite instruction, not a note** — every one of them has caught a real weakness in a hand-written narrative.

## REVISION ANALYSIS PRINCIPLE (read last, applies to everything above)

The skill is not a report of everything that changed in consensus.

It is an investment interpretation of what changed, what caused it, how durable it appears, what consensus now requires, and what could prove the current interpretation wrong.

When forced to choose between more data and better judgement, choose better judgement.
